SAMPLE SPOKEN PRESENTATION (about 6 minutes, adapt it to your own words)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
[Opening, 30 s, slide 1]
"Every PLM project starts almost from zero. The client has its own editor, its own vocabulary in two languages,
its own method. And the decisions that matter live in minutes, tickets and emails. A decision taken in one meeting
silently breaks an interface agreed in another, and we find out at test, when it is expensive. Our case is ACME,
an automotive supplier: the PLM part description is 80 characters, the ERP field holds 40. Agreed in one meeting,
contradicted in another, discovered at test. That is the failure Vertex Hub prevents."

[The idea, 40 s, slide 2]
"Vertex Hub is one sentence: the AI reads, the graph reasons, a person decides. Documents go through guardrails
first: personal data is masked and hidden instructions are quarantined. Then rules and a local model read the text,
and every fact must quote its source word for word, otherwise it is rejected. The facts land in a knowledge graph
built on one ontology, so Plant BOM, BOP and PBOM are the same thing. Nothing changes without a named person's approval."

[Demo 1, setup, 40 s]
"New client: ACME Mobility, 3DEXPERIENCE, SAP, MES, SAFe. Here is its backlog and its glossary, in French and English.
Now a second client, a medtech company on Windchill with no MES. The framework adapts: it adds requirements and
verification, and skips MES. Same tool, different client, no migration."

[Demo 2, the document, 60 s]
"Here is a French workshop minute: limit the part description to 40 characters. The tool maps 'designation article'
to Part Description and shows the exact sentence it relied on. On the left you can see what it masked before the
model saw anything. In the steering committee minutes a line tried to hijack the assistant. It was quarantined and
logged, and nothing was extracted from it."

[Demo 3, impact and trade-off, 80 s]
"This is the proposal card. Option A fixes the truncation on the PLM to ERP interface, but it outdates test TC-205
and breaks the naming rule in US-501. Five stories and four tests are directly impacted. The longer list through
interfaces is collapsed, because a board needs the direct impact first. And look at the alerts: the 80 versus 40
conflict was detectable on 21 September, two weeks before the test failed on 5 October."

[Demo 4, the decision, 40 s]
"I approve. Nine Jira drafts are prepared, and nothing is sent until I press Send. The decision is logged with its
full context. That is our answer to hallucination and to trust: the AI proposes, a person disposes."

[Demo 5, Ask Vertex, 60 s]
"Now I can ask questions. [Ask: Why are part descriptions truncated?] The answer cites graph items, and any citation
that does not exist is discarded. [Ask: Which requirements talk about availability?] This one comes from the public
PROMISE and PURE datasets, 625 labelled requirements and 79 real documents, cited as EXT identifiers.
[Ask the injection line.] And it cannot be hijacked: the model has no tools and cannot change anything."

[Honest numbers, 30 s]
"We measured, not claimed. On documents the rules were written for they score 8 out of 8, on a held-out document
they score 0 out of 4. The local model and the hybrid find 3 out of 4, each with a verbatim quote. Our injection
filter has 12 percent recall on a public benchmark, and we chose it on purpose: zero false alarms on real project
language, because a filter that blocks real minutes gets switched off. It is the first of several layers."

[Close, 30 s, slide 3]
"Build fee per client, a run subscription per project, support each year. It reads what teams already write, runs
locally so nothing leaves the site, and is reused across clients. We learned that vocabulary is the real
integration problem, and that you must measure on data you never tuned on. Thank you, we are happy to take questions."

If something fails on stage: say "the AI layer is optional, here it is on rules only", keep going, and show the
sidebar AI status. Never apologise at length; move to the next scene.
